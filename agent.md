# Agent content and map rules

- Keep page copy concise. A page gets one clear title; every other visible phrase must directly guide a user action, identify content, communicate status, or provide a necessary accessibility/safety/data limitation. Remove decorative slogans, redundant explanatory paragraphs, and filler metadata.
- For travel visit records, capture and display month and year only (`YYYY-MM`). Do not ask for or show a day. Preserve older saved `YYYY-MM-DD` records by migrating them to their month on read; prevent duplicate stamps within the same place and month.
- In the major-city layer, show a familiar three-letter city/airport code (for example PDX or NYC) beside the Chinese city name wherever a reliable code exists. Make codes comfortably readable, not tiny metadata. Keep English names searchable and accessible.
- Maps must identify major administrative divisions and geographic regions where relevant. Keep political/administrative boundaries distinct from travel-region groupings; do not imply approximate travel regions are official borders. Keep the map free of persistent place/region text overlays; show country, state, park, city, and regional names on hover, and retain clear regional controls. Document when boundaries or locations are approximate.
- Keep the travel atlas destination index populated and selectable alongside the map. Test initial rendering (including JavaScript errors), index availability, and actual map clicks so a missing required DOM element cannot silently disable both interactions and the index.
- Keep all 50 U.S. states, the existing 64 U.S. national parks, and 48 Canadian national parks in one North America layer. Retain the original `us:` and `ca:` storage keys so existing stamp records remain valid; allow filters for the U.S., Canada, and their mapped subregions.
