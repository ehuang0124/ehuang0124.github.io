Eva Huang

I'm an aspiring civic technologist, drawn to tools that make public services and civic life work for everyone.

I'm a software engineer at Salesforce and a part-time master's student at Cornell Tech, where my coursework covers tech, media and democracy, Internet law and policy, trust and safety, and public interest technology.

For six years I've worked on higher education access and equity. I co-founded Beyond Taiwan, a volunteer-led nonprofit that opens global higher education and career mentorship to Taiwanese public-school students, and served as its mentorship chair as the network grew past 500 members.

Lately I've been thinking about how physical AI could open up job access and education, helping people learn hands-on skills and find their way into work.

Projects

Bluesky job scam labeler

For Cornell Tech's Trust & Safety course, I built a Bluesky moderation labeler that flags recruitment fraud, impersonated employers, and deceptive job offers aimed at job seekers. Because people subscribe to labels instead of relying on takedowns, the project weighed how to warn users clearly while keeping false positives low.

Electronic voting machines and democratic accountability

For Cornell Tech's Internet Law and Policy course, I wrote a paper on U.S. electronic voting machines from 2000 to 2020, from the Florida recount through the Help America Vote Act and federal certification. It argues that new machines only earn public trust when voters can cast and check ballots on their own, including voters with disabilities, and when election offices can catch and correct errors.

Booking and payments for local businesses

As an early product manager at the startup WishMobile, I led a booking product from zero to launch. I mapped how neighborhood wellness and recreation shops ran their days, then replaced phone confirmations with booking and payment through Google Maps or the shop's own site. It reached 18% of platform revenue within three months.

Cloud infrastructure at Salesforce

I work on the API platform that serves more than 41 billion requests a day, on tenant isolation for AI and government cloud workloads, and on defending public-sector tenants from live DDoS attacks. I also led Mesh Analyzer, which turned one-off customer debugging into a reviewable policy any team can reuse.

Getting to the Kendeda Building

At Georgia Tech's Energy Institute, I studied how people reach the Kendeda Building, the campus's Living Building Challenge certified building. I combined interviews, GIS mapping of community destinations, and four personas so different mobility needs weren't averaged into one user.
